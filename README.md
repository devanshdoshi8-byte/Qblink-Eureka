# Qblink

Build a premium introductory website for a startup called Qblink. This is not a full SaaS product site yet. It is an early-stage startup landing website meant to explain the idea clearly, build trust, collect interest, and show that the product is real and serious.





Brand and style





Use a clean modern SaaS design

White background with soft blue accents

Rounded cards, subtle shadows, smooth spacing

Elegant sans-serif typography

Mobile-first, responsive layout

Minimal, premium, professional, and trustworthy

Add the Qblink logo wherever appropriate; I will upload it separately

Do not use any platform watermark

Do not make the UI look childish, noisy, or overly futuristic







Main product idea





Qblink turns chaotic walk-in traffic into a live digital flow. Customers can join a queue from anywhere using a QR code or link, see their real-time position and wait time, and get notified when their turn is near. Businesses manage the queue from a simple dashboard. The product removes physical queue chaos, reduces crowd frustration, and gives businesses visibility and control without hardware.





Website type





This is an introductory website, not a full payment or heavy backend website.

The goal is:



explain the product,

show the problem,

show how it works,

show sector-specific use cases,

collect leads through a demo / early access form,

present affiliate opportunity,

build credibility.

Qblink is not a generic queue management app. It is a hardware-free digital flow system for walk-in businesses.



The core idea is to convert physical waiting lines into a remote, trackable, and predictable experience.





How it works in real life:





In a typical business like a clinic, restaurant, or bank:



Customers arrive and see a long queue

They don’t know how long the wait is

They keep asking staff repeatedly

Some get frustrated and leave

Staff manages everything manually using paper or memory





This creates chaos, stress, and revenue loss.





What Qblink changes:





Instead of standing in line:



Customers scan a QR code or open a link

They join the queue digitally

They can see their live position and estimated wait time

They can wait anywhere (not physically in line)

They get notified when their turn is near





For businesses:



They get a simple dashboard

They can call next customer, add walk-ins, or manage flow

They can control multiple counters

They get basic data like queue size and wait time







Key philosophy:





Qblink is about:



reducing visible chaos

removing uncertainty

preventing customer drop-offs

giving businesses control over walk-ins





It is NOT:

built for real-world usage
